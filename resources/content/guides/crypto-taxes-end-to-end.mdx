## Three numbers, in order

A crypto tax estimate is a short chain. You start from what you paid — your **cost basis**. You measure how long you held, because the **holding period** decides whether a gain is taxed at the higher short-term rate or the lower long-term one. Then you apply the rate to the gain to get the **tax owed**.

Get the order right and the arithmetic is simple. Get it wrong and you can misjudge a bill by the entire difference between the two rate bands.

## Put it together

The capital-gains calculator runs the whole chain: proceeds minus basis is the gain, the days you held pick the rate, and the tax comes out with your net proceeds beside it.

## The supporting numbers

Two more numbers feed the estimate above. When you have bought in several lots, work out a single **cost basis** and average per-unit basis first. When a sale is close to the one-year line, check the **holding period** to see whether waiting moves you into long-term treatment.

If you only want the plain gain or loss against a live price — before any tax treatment — the profit calculator is the quickest read. Prices it shows carry their source and as-of time, so you know exactly when each figure was observed.
